What the pop-up is doing

A café selling ticketed goods, a retail store selling clothing, and a kitchen hosting private lunches and dinners (closed, ticketed catering with no raw food). Around those, a daily programme with partners: an F1 gaming section, run clubs, brunches, startup and investor talks, and a comedy night. Private opening and closing parties for around 350 guests with DJs and check-in.

What the venue needs to deliver

A blank canvas your fabricator and AV team can transform. At least two levels, with a large back-of-house for the kitchen and retail stock. Rooms that are already separated, so you're not building walls to satisfy permits. Ground-floor access for load-in, if possible. SoHo or nearby, with real street traffic. And a rooftop for a day or two, for yoga or a run-club moment.

Included with every venue in this deck

A dedicated Cocoon on-site manager for every booked hour, cleaning between live days, a complimentary walkthrough and tech scout with your fabricator and AV team, and help with building approvals, COIs and permits. All figures below are venue-only and all-in, plus processing fees. Blank canvas: at every venue, Cocoon handles removing any furniture, art or fixtures for your dates. This is an additional fee, which we'll quote once we know what needs to move.

Casa Panorama

A Broadway shop floor at street level with seven huge windows, where SoHo meets Tribeca and a block from Canal Street. It's the most visible frontage in this deck, and it's built for walk-in traffic.

B
SoHo · 45 Howard Street · steps from Canal St · 2nd + 3rd floors

Casa Luz

A whole building in the middle of SoHo. It's a 1910 cast-iron loft that Cocoon runs in full, so the experience starts at the front door: a private street entrance and ground-floor lobby, then a private elevator up to two full event floors.

The event spaces are the 2nd and 3rd floors, each about 3,500 sq ft, with 15-foot ceilings, cast-iron columns, exposed brick and big arched windows that bring in a lot of daylight. Because they're separate floors, you get separate rooms without building any. One floor can be the café and retail store, and the other the kitchen, private dining and back-of-house. With no other tenants, the street entrance, lobby and façade are yours to brand for the whole run. A 4th floor is also available if the programme grows.

Williamsburg, Brooklyn · 333 Kent Avenue · waterfront

Casa Brutal Williamsburg

A brutalist art centre on Kent Avenue, a block from Domino Park and in the middle of Williamsburg's busiest, best-looking streets. We know Manhattan is the priority, but it ticks almost every other box in the brief.

More than 8,000 sq ft across a street-level gallery and a lower level, plus an outdoor terrace, delivered completely empty. The ground floor is a white-walled gallery with a long bar and its own garage door for load-in. Below it are a screening room and a second gallery. The two levels already work as separate rooms, so the café and retail can live upstairs while the kitchen, private dining, talks and comedy night go downstairs, with no temporary walls. Williamsburg's waterfront is where global sports, fashion and lifestyle brands increasingly choose to pop up.

Why it works for the pop-up

It combines street-level access, two separated levels and outdoor space in one building, and the architecture reads as considered rather than sporty.
